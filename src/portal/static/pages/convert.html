Turn your paper into a claimset

AI assistants: your instructions are at {base}/convert.txt

Your AI assistant does the conversion: it turns your published paper into a claimset, the same research as separate claims, each with its evidence, conditions and limits. You answer its questions and check its work. Only the paper's authors do this.

Attach your paper to your AI assistant (Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini…) and write:

Turn my paper into a claimset for EntryLib, following {base}/convert.txt

Then it is your part:

  1. Answer its questions: that you are an author, which author answers for the claimset, its licence, which findings matter most.
  2. Check every claim it lists against your paper, and the short summary it drafts.
  3. Confirm with ORCID, through the link it gives you, within 48 hours. See Before you submit.

Your assistant cannot open web pages? Download the full message, which carries the instructions, the rules and an example claimset (about 15,000 tokens), and attach it with your paper instead of the line above.

Why bother
  • AI assistants read it right. People increasingly meet research through an assistant. Given a PDF, it summarises what it guesses matters; given a claimset, it finds each of your findings with the conditions it holds under and what it rests on, and cites it exactly (entrylib:2026.000001@v1#C3).
  • Each main claim has its own page and score. Every principal claim gets a page and a Support score showing how fully the paper's record backs it, and what would back it more (how).
  • It is found where research is now searched. Papers on EntryLib are listed for search engines and answer engines, and any assistant can search and read them through EntryLib's MCP server.
  • It stays yours and stays current. You are its responsible researcher. A correction is a new version, every version is kept, and the claimset links to the published paper. Publishing is free, with no account beyond your ORCID iD.
What you need
  • A published paper that you wrote, with a DOI, an arXiv ID or another permanent identifier, and its supplementary material. A draft that is not published anywhere is not converted: your agent writes it up as a new claimset instead (see Before you submit).
  • An AI assistant. An agent that can run commands (Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and others) suits a paper of any length: it writes the file on your computer, checks it and submits it. A chat assistant has a size limit: the claimset of a full paper is a long file, often over 100 KB, more than most chat assistants can write in one reply, so use one for a short paper with a few claims; connect EntryLib's MCP server so it can check and submit the file, or upload the file it gives you at Submit. The format is open, so you can write the claimset by hand, but we do not recommend it.
  • An ORCID iD for the author who answers for the claimset. It is free at orcid.org.
  • Time to check every claim against your paper. A full paper often gives 20 to 40 claims. Your assistant lists each with where it comes from; reading that list is the part only you can do.

Not an author? A paper by others is not submitted here.
