claimset-lab

Research with your agent that anyone can check

claimset-lab keeps a record of your research while it happens: what you asked, what you planned before you saw any data, every run of code with its inputs and outputs, every decision and every result, including the ones that did not work. Your agent writes it as it works; at the end it becomes a claimset whose claims trace back to the record.

Get started

See a finished example, each with its record linked under the title: a quantitative study (the accuracy of a forecasting method) and a qualitative one (what users of a music program ask for).

The problem

An agent can run a hundred analyses in an afternoon and report the one that worked. Nothing in the finished paper shows whether a test was planned or chosen after the fact, how many attempts came before the reported one, or whether a number came from code or from memory. Readers, and the models that read for them, have to take it on trust.

claimset-lab makes those things part of the record, so they can be checked instead of trusted.

What the record makes checkable

Planned before tested

A test counts as confirmatory only if its plan, with the significance level, sample and the rule for each outcome, was recorded and pushed before the run. Anything else is reported as exploration.

Every number from code

Each run records its command, code commit, environment, seeds, parameters, input-file hashes and outputs. Results enter the record from the files the code wrote, never retyped.

Failures counted

Refuted and abandoned hypotheses stay in the record with their reasons. A planned test gets one attempt, so it cannot be rerun until it comes out right. Negative results are published like positive ones.

Hard to rewrite

The record is append-only and hash-chained, kept on its own git branch and pushed after every step. Corrections are new entries that point at what they replace; nothing is edited in place.

People decide

Every actor is registered: agents with their model, people with their ORCID iD. A supervisor's approval of a plan is recorded in their own words, with when they gave it; an agent never approves its own plan.

Checks by others

Another agent or a colleague can reproduce runs and re-derive results in their own log. Their verdicts, confirmed or disputed, travel with the claims they checked.

How it works

Your agent runs the commands as it works; you approve plans and answer its questions.

  1. Frame: questions, hypotheses with what they are about, the terms the research uses, the assumptions it makes.
  2. Plan: design, analysis and decision rule, fixed before the data is seen, and approved by the supervisor when there is one.
  3. Run and record: exploratory and confirmatory runs, deviations from the plan, decisions, and the verdict the plan's rule gives.
  4. Export and publish: the record becomes a claimset: hypotheses become claims, plans and runs their evidence. Submit it to EntryLib with the record attached.

From exploration to a planned test

Your agent runsThe record gets
claimset lab hypothesis "M helps most at horizons 7–12." --from-exploration --prompted-by RN1 H2, marked as suggested by RN1
claimset lab plan --kind confirmatory --hypotheses H2 --supported "p < alpha and d > 0" … PL1, pushed before any run
claimset lab run --plan PL1 -- python3 confirm.py --holdoutRN2, with results RS2–RS3
claimset lab verdict PL1p = 0.004, d = 0.031: supported

Beyond computation

The lab records any research that can be planned and logged: experiments, simulations, benchmarks, data analyses, and research on documents and other sources. Data you collect yourself, such as measurements, survey responses or observations, enter the record as files: each is captured and fingerprinted when it arrives, and the analysis that reads it is run as code, so every reported number still traces to a recorded run. What one kind of work needs beyond that comes as an extension. The qualitative extension records sources as they were read, codes assigned to passages with the rules for each code, and counts that a planned test can use; the record then shows that the sampling was fixed before the coding, and the coding before the counting.

What readers see

A paper from the lab is labelled Made with the lab and links to its record. Each claim says whether it was planned, under which plan and runs, and what deviated from the plan. Readers and models can tell a tested prediction from a pattern found by looking.

Get started

claimset-lab is a command-line tool and a set of instructions for research agents. Free and open source.

What you need

  • An AI agent that can run commands in your project, such as Claude Code, Codex, Cursor or Gemini CLI. A chat assistant in a browser cannot keep a record; it can help you plan the study. You can type the lab's commands yourself, without an agent, but we do not recommend it: the lab is built for an agent to keep the record while you make the decisions.
  • Python 3.10 or later and git. The tool needs nothing else.
  • A git repository with a remote you can push to, on GitHub or a similar host. The record is pushed after every step, and that push is what shows a plan came before its results. Without a remote, a study can be kept in desk mode: the record is still complete, but its plans are not dated by anyone else, and readers are told so.
  • An ORCID iD, to publish the result here. It is free at orcid.org.

Three steps

  1. Install the tool
    pip install claimset
  2. Give your agent the instructions, from your project's folder
    claimset init
  3. Ask your agent to start a lab record
    "Start a lab record for this research and keep it as we work."

What your agent will ask you

The agent does the recording; the decisions are yours. Expect to be asked for:

  • your name and ORCID iD, and a short name for the study;
  • the question, and what you expect to find, before any result is seen;
  • your approval of each plan before it is run, in your own words: the test, the data, and the rule that decides the outcome;
  • a decision whenever the work has to depart from a plan;
  • at the end, a check of every claim against the results, the licence, the findings to put first, and your confirmation by signing in with ORCID.

Plan a study with any assistant

Before the research starts, any assistant can help you plan it, a chat assistant included: what to measure, which test, which design fits the study, and why. It works from EntryLib's good practice for planning a study: it proposes a default with its reason and the paper it rests on, asks you about the rest, and ends with the agreed plan and where each choice came from.

{plan_request}

Paste it into a conversation with your assistant, then describe your study. It tells the assistant where to read the guides: EntryLib's connector, the web or a terminal.

Your assistant can use none of these? Download the full message, which carries every guide (about 10,000 tokens), and attach the file.

A plan agreed in a conversation is a reasoned design with its sources, not a record: nothing in it shows when it was made, or that it came before the data. To make that checkable, do the research with an agent that can run commands and claimset-lab (above); the agreed plan becomes the first plan of its record.
