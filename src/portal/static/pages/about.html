About EntryLib

Most research reaches readers as PDFs written for people to read from start to finish. More and more of it is now read first by AI, which has to guess where a finding is, what it rests on and how far it holds. EntryLib publishes research so that nothing has to be guessed: each finding is stated on its own, with its evidence, its assumptions and its limits, for people and for AI alike.

What a paper on EntryLib is

Every paper on EntryLib is written as a claimset, an open format for papers written as claims. Each claim says one thing and points to what supports it, the data, the proof or the experiment, and to what it assumes and where it may not hold. Its terms are defined, its numbers come in tables, and the earlier work it builds on is linked. The format is open and documented in the Claimset specification.

Words used on a paper's page

Claim (C1, C2…)
One finding of the paper, stated exactly, with the conditions it holds under.
Principal claim
One of the paper's main findings, the ones it puts first.
Evidence (E1, E2…)
What a claim rests on: a result, a table, a proof, an experiment, or a cited paper's finding.
Argument (W1, W2…)
The reasoning that links evidence to a claim, when the link is not plain.
Assumption (A1, A2…)
What must hold for a claim to follow; some are checked in the paper, some are not.
Definition (D1, D2…)
A term as the paper uses it. Underlined terms show their definitions.
Scope and conditions
Where a claim holds: a data set, a population, a range of settings.
Limitation
Where the authors think a claim may not hold.
Artefact
Material behind the paper: data, code, a manuscript, a figure.
Support
A score from 0 to 100 for how well the paper's own record backs a claim (Scores).
Evidence, Plan, Checkable, Context, Open problems
The five parts of a Support score, one coloured dot each, with the points lost in it. Click a claim's dots to see why.
Responsible researcher
The person who answers for the paper; in the format and the files, its guarantor.
Version
Each published state of a paper, never changed; a correction is a new version.
Claimset
The open format every paper here is written in, and its file.

One format for every field, with extensions

The core of the format is what research in every field shares: claims, their evidence, assumptions and limits, and the terms they use. That is what lets a model compare findings across fields. What only one kind of work needs is added by an extension: the glosses of examples in linguistics, the coding of sources in qualitative research. A paper names the extensions it uses, and stays complete without them: a reader that does not know an extension still reads every claim. Extensions are specified and versioned like the core. Today there are two, linguistics and qualitative research; more come as fields need them.

Where papers come from

  • Made with the lab Written from a record of the research kept while it was done, with claimset-lab. The record is linked from the paper.
  • From the authors' paper Prepared by its authors, usually with an AI agent, from a paper they wrote, which it links (how).
  • Written as a claimset Written directly as a claimset, usually with an AI agent.
  • From a published paper Converted from a paper published elsewhere, which it links; one of that paper's authors answers for the conversion.

Who answers for a paper

Every paper has a responsible researcher, called its guarantor: a named person who answers for it. Before anything is published, they confirm it by signing in with their ORCID iD. Nobody can publish in someone else's name, and an agent cannot publish on its own.

"Guarantor" is the format's technical term, used in the specification, the files and the API. On EntryLib's pages the guarantor is called the responsible researcher: the two words name the same person. Agents that wrote the paper are listed as what it was "written with". To submit, see Before you submit.

What EntryLib checks, and what it does not

Every submission is checked against the specification: its claims, support and references must fit together. Its text is screened for content aimed at manipulating AI readers. A lab record is checked for tampering, and each claim is scored by public rules.

Scores

Support, from 0 to 100, says how far a paper's own record supports each claim. It checks the record, not the science: it does not say whether a claim is true, and it is not peer review. (The rubric calls it reliability.) Every claim starts at 100 and loses points for named problems: weak evidence, a test not planned beforehand, material a reader cannot obtain, a missing scope or link to earlier work, open limitations and unchecked assumptions. Five dots beside each claim show where the points went: evidence, plan, checkable, context and open problems; click them for the claim's breakdown. A paper's score is the mean of its principal claims; beside it, each part shows the points its principal claims lost there on average, and “Why” names the lowest claim. Well supported is 80 and over, Partly supported 60 to 79, Weakly supported under 60. Click a score to see why. The rules are in the rubric.

Novelty is the authors' own account (new, extends, replicates or refutes earlier work), checked against the claims already on EntryLib: a close match the paper does not acknowledge puts it in question.

The scores are computed by rules from the paper's file. They check the record, not the science, and may change as the rules and reviews develop. The rules are public: the EntryLib rubric.

Citing, versions and corrections

Every paper has a permanent identifier, such as entrylib:2026.000001, and each version its own, such as entrylib:2026.000001@v2. Cite a claim by adding its number: entrylib:2026.000001@v2#C4. A published version is never changed. A correction is a new version, and a problem with a paper is shown in a notice at its top (retraction and removal).

Open by design

Reading, searching and publishing are free. Papers carry open licences chosen by their guarantors. Every published version is also kept in a public registry on GitHub, and the specification, the tools and this site's code are open source.

If EntryLib stopped, its papers would not be lost: every published version, with its notices, is in the public registry under the licence its guarantor chose, and its identifiers can be resolved from it.

Who runs EntryLib

EntryLib is new. It opened on 9 November 2026, and in its first weeks most papers on it are guaranteed by Ivan Svetunkov, who runs it. Judge them as you would any other: by their claims and what each rests on.

EntryLib is not yet a company. Until it is, Ivan Svetunkov runs it personally and is responsible for it: for the site and its rules, for what is published and for every decision about a paper after publication. He decides retractions, removals and expressions of concern, and disputes about a paper or its scores, following the published retraction and removal policy, and says on the paper's page what was decided and why. The rules that check and score papers are public, and a change to them is a new, published version, never a quiet edit.

To raise a problem with a paper, or with a decision, write to mail@entrylib.org.
