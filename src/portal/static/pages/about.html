About EntryLib

Most research reaches readers as PDFs written for people to read from start to finish. More and more of it is now read first by AI, which has to guess where a finding is, what it rests on and how far it holds. EntryLib publishes research so that nothing has to be guessed: each finding is stated on its own, with its evidence, its assumptions and its limits, for people and for AI alike.

What an entry is

An entry is a claimset: a paper written as claims. Each claim says one thing and points to what supports it, the data, the proof or the experiment, and to what it assumes and where it may not hold. Its terms are defined, its numbers come in tables, and the earlier work it builds on is linked. The format is open and documented in the Claimset specification.

One format for every field, with extensions

The core of the format is what research in every field shares: claims, their evidence, assumptions and limits, and the terms they use. That is what lets a model compare findings across fields. What only one kind of work needs is added by an extension: the glosses of examples in linguistics, the coding of sources in qualitative research. An entry names the extensions it uses, and stays complete without them: a reader that does not know an extension still reads every claim. Extensions are specified and versioned like the core. Today there are two, linguistics and qualitative research; more come as fields need them.

Where entries come from

  • lab Written from a record of the research kept while it was done, with claimset-lab. The record is linked from the entry.
  • native Written directly as a claimset, usually with an AI agent.
  • converted Made from a paper already published elsewhere, which it links.

Who answers for an entry

Every entry has a guarantor: a named researcher who answers for it. Before anything is published, the guarantor confirms it by signing in with their ORCID iD. Nobody can publish in someone else's name, and an agent cannot publish on its own.

What EntryLib checks, and what it does not

Every submission is checked against the specification: its claims, support and references must fit together. Its text is screened for content aimed at manipulating AI readers. A lab record is checked for tampering, and each claim shows whether its support can be checked by a reader:

  • Checkable: supported by evidence in the claimset that a reader can check.
  • Supported, not checkable: the data is restricted, or the support is cited work, argument or expert judgement.
  • Unsupported: no stated support, or support nobody can check.

Assumptions have no light: they are taken as given.

EntryLib does not referee research. Publishing here is not peer review, and an entry is as good as the evidence it shows. That is why every entry shows it.

Quality score

Each entry has a place for a quality score. How it is computed is still being worked out and will be published here; until then every entry shows –.

Citing, versions and corrections

Every entry has a permanent identifier, such as entrylib:2026.000001, and each version its own, such as entrylib:2026.000001@v2. Cite a claim by adding its number: entrylib:2026.000001@v2#C4. A published version is never changed. A correction is a new version, and a problem with an entry is shown in a notice at its top (retraction and removal).

Open by design

Reading, searching and publishing are free. Entries carry open licences chosen by their guarantors. Every published version is also kept in a public registry on GitHub, and the specification, the tools and this site's code are open source.
